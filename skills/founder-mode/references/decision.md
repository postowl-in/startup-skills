# Playbook: Kill the Decision Spiral

Trigger: "should I do A or B", "I can't decide", "is this the right call", build-vs-buy, pivot-or-persist, raise-or-bootstrap, fire-or-coach.

Purpose: the founder has usually been circling this decision for days. The job is not a balanced analysis — it's to collapse the spiral into a call in ten minutes. A good-enough decision made now beats the perfect decision made in three weeks, *except* when the decision is irreversible — so classify reversibility first.

## The three lenses

Run the decision through exactly these three, in order:

1. **What breaks if you're wrong.** Separate the two failure directions (chose A, should've chosen B — and the reverse). For each: how bad, how fast do you find out, and can you undo it. This classifies the decision as **reversible** (decide fast, the cost of deciding wrong is lower than the cost of deciding slowly) or **one-way-door** (the only kind that earns more deliberation time).

2. **What you're optimizing for vs. what you should be.** Read the founder's framing for the hidden objective — comfort, optics, avoiding a hard conversation, sunk cost, "keeping options open". Name the mismatch bluntly. This lens is where most spirals actually live: the decision is hard because the stated criteria aren't the real ones.

3. **What a founder who already made this mistake would tell you.** Channel the postmortem voice — the person 18 months downstream of the wrong call. What does that person know that's invisible from here? This is where the second-order effects surface: the hire you didn't fire poisons the next two hires; the feature you built for one big customer becomes the roadmap's permanent hostage.

## Output structure

ALWAYS use this template:

```
Here's what I'd actually do: [the call — A or B, plainly, no "it depends" opener]

## 1. What breaks if you're wrong
[both failure directions, time-to-discovery, undo cost]
→ This is a [reversible / one-way-door] decision. [implication for speed]

## 2. What you're optimizing for vs. what you should be
[the hidden objective in their framing, named directly]

## 3. What a founder who made this mistake would tell you
[the postmortem voice — 1 short paragraph, specific to this decision]

## The call
[Restate the decision + the ONE condition that would flip it. Not three
conditions — one. "Do A, unless X turns out true, in which case B."]

**Next 48 hours:** [the action that makes the decision real — send the email,
cancel the contract, post the job. Spirals reopen until an irreversible-ish
step is taken.]
```

## Quality bar

- Make the call. "Both options have merit" is a banned ending. If genuinely 50/50, say "it's a coin flip, which means speed wins — pick A because it's reversible faster" — that is still a call.
- Don't tell them what they want to hear. If the framing smells like they've already decided and want permission, name that, then evaluate the decision on its merits anyway.
- The flip-condition must be observable and near-term, not "if circumstances change."
- Respect the one-way-door exception: for genuinely irreversible calls (cofounder equity, company sale, firing a third of the team), slow down, insist on the missing data, and say what they need to learn before deciding.
