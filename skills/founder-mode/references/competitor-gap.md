# Playbook: Competitor Gap

Trigger: "my competitor is X", "how do I compete with / beat X", "find their weakness", "positioning against X".

Core frame: you are not trying to beat the competitor — you are trying to make them **irrelevant to a specific customer segment**. The gap worth owning is the one the competitor *structurally cannot close* because closing it would hurt their existing business (pricing model, core customer base, sales motion, or technical architecture locks them out).

## Before answering

Identify: who the competitor's *best* customer is, and who their *worst-served* customer is. The user's wedge almost always lives with the worst-served. If the user hasn't said who their own target customer is, ask — a positioning gap only exists relative to a customer.

If the competitor is publicly known, use what you know about them; if they're obscure or you're unsure, say so and build the analysis from the user's description rather than inventing facts about a company you can't verify.

## Output structure

ALWAYS use this template:

```
Here's what I'd actually do: [the one positioning move, stated up front]

## Their weaknesses
[2-3 real weaknesses. For each: WHY it exists structurally — legacy pricing,
enterprise focus, investor pressure, technical debt. A weakness without a
structural reason is just a feature gap they'll patch next quarter.]

## The gap you can own
[ONE positioning gap. Spell out: which customer segment feels it, why the
competitor can't close it without damaging their existing business, and the
one-sentence positioning statement the user could put on their homepage.]

## 3 moves to make them irrelevant (ranked by speed of execution)
1. **[Fastest — days to weeks]** [move + why it works + what it costs]
2. **[Medium — 1-3 months]** [move + why it works + what it costs]
3. **[Slowest — 3-12 months]** [the structural moat move]

## What they'll do when they notice you
[One paragraph: the competitor's most likely response, and why your position
survives it — or what you must have built by then for it to survive.]

**Next 48 hours:** [concrete first step on move #1]
```

## Quality bar

- Each weakness needs its structural "why". "Their UI is dated" is worthless; "their UI is dated because their revenue is enterprise contracts sold top-down, so they have no incentive to fix self-serve onboarding — which is exactly the door for you" is a real gap.
- Moves must be ranked by speed, and the fastest one must be executable by a small team without new funding.
- "Make them irrelevant" means: after the move, your target customer no longer puts you and the competitor in the same comparison set. Test each move against that bar.
- Never recommend cloning their roadmap. If the best move you can find is "do what they do, cheaper", say plainly that there's no defensible gap and the user should pick a different wedge or a different competitor.
