# Playbook: A-Player Filter

Trigger: "hiring my first X", "interview questions for X", "how do I know if they're good", first-10-employees hiring decisions.

Core frame: early hires are evaluated on a different axis than corporate hires. The question is not "are they qualified" — it's "do they create motion without supervision in ambiguity". Interview-polish correlates weakly (sometimes negatively) with that. The playbook's job: questions that can't be prepped for, and signals readable in the first 30 minutes.

## What makes a question separate A-players from good interviewers

A filtering question has these properties — generate questions for the specific role with these in mind, don't recycle a fixed list:

- **It asks for past specifics, not hypotheticals.** "Walk me through the last time you shipped something nobody asked you to" beats "how do you handle ambiguity". A-players have a library of specifics; polished interviewers have frameworks.
- **It has a follow-up that breaks rehearsed answers.** Ask for the second example. The first story is prepared; the second one is the truth.
- **It makes them argue against themselves.** "What was wrong with the approach you just described?" A-players critique their own work fluently; B-players defend.
- **It probes what they did when something was on fire and no one assigned them.** First hires at a startup spend half their time outside the job description.
- **It surfaces their actual bar.** "Tell me about a piece of work you shipped that you weren't proud of, and what you did about it." People with high standards have a vivid answer; people without have a deflection.

## Output structure

ALWAYS use this template:

```
Here's what I'd actually do: [the single most important thing to screen for in
THIS role at THIS stage — one sentence]

## 5 questions that separate A-players from good interviewers
[For each: the question, what an A-player answer sounds like, what a
polished-B answer sounds like, and the follow-up that splits them.
Tailor all five to the specific role — a first sales hire and a first
engineer get different questions.]

## The month-3 red flag — and how to spot it in 30 minutes
[ONE red flag, specific to this role, that founders typically discover at
month 3. Explain the mechanism (why it stays hidden through interviews and
honeymoon) and the interview-time probe that exposes it early. This is the
flagship section — make it sharp.]

## What the first 30 minutes tell you
[2-3 behavioral signals readable in the conversation itself: do they ask
about the business or just the role; do they give numbers unprompted; do
their stories have an "I" with agency or a vague "we".]

**Next 48 hours:** [concrete step — e.g., add a paid trial-project stage,
rewrite the JD around outcomes, send the take-home that mirrors real work]
```

## Quality bar

- All five questions tailored to the named role. A question that works equally for a sales hire and a backend engineer is too generic to filter anyone.
- The month-3 red flag must come with its **mechanism** — why it survives the interview process — or the founder can't generalize it to the next hire.
- Recommend a work-sample or paid trial whenever the role allows; interviews predict interviews, work predicts work. Say this even if the user only asked for questions.
- If the user is hiring to delegate something they've never done themselves, flag the classic trap: you can't evaluate or manage a function you've never run — suggest the founder do it badly for 30 days first, or hire a fractional expert to set the bar.
