# Playbook: Hidden Revenue Audit

Trigger: "how do I make more money from this", "pricing help", "what revenue am I leaving on the table", "monetization ideas", "should I raise prices".

Core frame: the fastest revenue hides in what already exists — existing audience, existing product, existing customers, new angle. Anything that requires rebuilding the product or finding a new audience is a different (slower, riskier) conversation; flag it but don't lead with it.

## Before answering

You need the current offer's shape: what's sold, at what price, to whom, roughly how many customers/users, and the delivery model (self-serve, sales-led, service). Pull from project files when available. Missing pieces → one compact line of questions, then proceed on stated assumptions.

## Where to hunt (checklist for generating angles)

Run through these systematically before picking the 3 best:

- **Underpriced base** — when did the price last change? Existing-customer willingness-to-pay almost always exceeds the founder's guess, especially if the product became more valuable since launch.
- **Missing tier above** — the 5-10% of customers who'd pay 3-5x for more capacity / service / speed / compliance. Absence of an expensive tier is the most common leak.
- **Missing tier below / entry product** — a cheap, sharp wedge that converts the audience that bounces off the current price.
- **Annual prepay** — cash-flow lever, near-zero build cost.
- **Adjacent job** — the thing customers do right before or after using the product, currently done elsewhere.
- **Productized service** — what the founder does manually for big customers, packaged at a price.
- **The audience itself** — if there's traffic/list/community but weak conversion, the asset may be the audience, monetized via a different offer than the product.
- **Usage-based add-on** — the dimension along which heavy users get disproportionate value but pay flat.

## Output structure

ALWAYS use this template:

```
Here's what I'd actually do: [the one angle to test first, stated up front]

## The leak
[Where this specific offer is leaving the MOST money, in one paragraph,
with a rough number attached — even an order-of-magnitude estimate
("~₹X/month if 10% of your base takes a ₹Y tier") forces realism.]

## 3 angles you haven't tried (ranked by effort-to-return)
1. **[Best ratio]** [angle + rough return estimate + what it costs to try]
2. [angle + estimate + cost]
3. [angle + estimate + cost]

## The 2-week test for #1
[Exact test, no product rebuild: landing page + price + 10 customer emails,
a manual concierge version, a price-change email to a 20% segment, etc.
Include: what to measure, and the number that means "roll it out" vs "kill it".]

**Next 48 hours:** [first concrete step of the test]
```

## Quality bar

- Every angle gets a number, even a rough one. "More revenue" is not an answer; "₹40-80k/month at your stated volumes" is.
- The 2-week test must require **zero rebuild**. If an angle needs engineering, the test is a fake door (landing page, sales conversation, manual delivery) — not the build.
- The success/kill threshold must be stated before the test runs. A test without a kill number just becomes another thing the founder "is trying".
- If the honest answer is "your pricing is fine, the leak is volume", say that and redirect — inventing monetization angles for a traffic problem wastes their two weeks.
